ARTICLE · ABOUT 7 MINUTES · DRAFT

Build Your Brand: Your Story, Told the Same Way Everywhere

By Coach Stack · College Recruiting Standard

Draft for Coach Stack's review. New content written for Lesson 8, built on the framework's Step 8 actions and O.W.N. ID.

Coaches meet you before they meet you

Long before a college coach watches you play in person, they'll meet you through a screen: your recruiting profile, your highlight video, your email, maybe your social media. In those first few minutes, they're deciding whether you're worth a closer look.

That's your brand. Not a logo or a slogan, but your story, told the same way everywhere. And the good news is that you've already done most of the hard work.

Start with who you are

Your brand starts with the self-knowledge you built in Level II: your strengths, your why and your identity statement. In O.W.N. ID you'll pull it together in four strands:

StrandThe question
Who I amThree words that describe you, your identity statement and your why
What I valueThe three to five things that matter most to you
What I bringYour strengths, including your Sport Wheel from Step 7
What I'm looking forThe kind of college, team and coach you want

Pick your three words carefully. If a coach met you, watched you play and talked to your teammates, would they use the same three words? That's the test of an honest brand.

Build your five assets

Your brand shows up in five places. Build each one so it tells the same story:

  1. Your recruiting profile. A short bio, your grad year and position, your academics and your schedule.
  2. Your intro letter. A short email in your own words that introduces you to a coach. You'll start sending these in Step 9.
  3. Your highlight video. Your best clips first, with you easy to spot. Ask your coach which clips to use, and what college coaches in your sport like to see, since some also want full-game film.
  4. Your social media. Coaches look. Make sure what they find matches who you are.
  5. Your 30-second pitch. What you say when a coach asks, “Tell me about yourself.”

Same story everywhere

If your profile says you're a 2029 center back and your video says 2028 midfielder, a coach notices. Use the same photo, the same name, grad year and position, the same bio, a current video link and the same numbers on every profile. Consistency says you're organized, and organized players are easier to recruit.

Audit your social media

Many college coaches check recruits' social media, and what they find can end the conversation before it starts. Go through every account with fresh eyes. Your photo, your handle, every post, photos you're tagged in, what you like and repost. If you wouldn't want a coach, a teacher or your grandmother to see it, take it down. Then ask someone you trust to look too.

Social media can also help you: a public account that shows your training, your team and your character is part of your brand. Redo the audit every season.

Practice your 30-second pitch

At a camp, a showcase or on the phone, a coach will eventually say, “Tell me about yourself.” Have an answer ready: who you are, what you bring, what you're working on and what you're looking for. Practice it out loud at least three times, with a parent, a coach and a friend, until it sounds like you talking, not a script.

Close out Level IV: retake the Wheel

Step 8 ends Level IV, so it's time for your third Wheel of Recruiting Success. Retake it and compare the new shape with your Baseline and your Level II take. You'll see how far you've come since freshman year.

Your moves for Step 8

  1. Write three words that describe who you are.
  2. Make your recruiting profiles match your brand.
  3. Draft your intro letter in your own words.
  4. Film your highlight video with your coach's input.
  5. Audit your social media and practice your pitch.

10Family conversation guide Take-home · 15 min

Dear family, in Step 8 your student-athlete builds their brand: their story, told the same way everywhere a coach might find them. They'll build five assets (a recruiting profile, an intro letter, a highlight video, a clean social media presence and a 30-second pitch) and retake the Wheel to close out Level IV.

The most important idea: a brand isn't a persona. It's who your student really is, told consistently.

Do this together

  1. Be one of the three people your student practices their 30-second pitch with.
  2. Look through their social media together, with fresh eyes, and without judgment.
  3. Help with the highlight video: filming, a ride to games, or asking the coach for footage.

Your role this step: let your student write their own intro letter and bio. Coaches can tell when a parent wrote it. Review it for spelling, not for voice.

Check your own accounts too: coaches sometimes notice what parents post about the team.

11Coach talking points For head coaches · 5 min read

Say these three things

  1. “Your brand is your story, told the same way everywhere a coach might find you.”
  2. “College coaches look at social media. Make sure what they find matches who you are.”
  3. “Come to me for your highlight clips. I'll help you pick the ones that show what coaches want to see.”

Questions you'll get

QuestionAnswer
“Coach, which clips should I use?”Pick clips that show their strengths at their position, best one first, and say if college coaches in your sport also want full-game film.
“Can you listen to my pitch?”Yes. That's one of their three practices. Give one thing that worked and one to sharpen.
“Can I put you down as a contact?”Agree on how college coaches should reach you, and what you'll say.
“Should I delete my account?”Usually no. Clean it up so it shows who they are.
