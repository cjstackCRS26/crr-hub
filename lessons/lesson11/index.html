ARTICLE · ABOUT 7 MINUTES · DRAFT

Visit Campus: See the Campus You'd Live On

By Coach Stack · College Recruiting Standard

Draft for Coach Stack's review. New content written for Lesson 11, built on the framework's Step 11 actions and the dashboard's Campus Visit Scorecard. Visit dates were checked in November 2026; confirm your sport's dates each year.

A visit shows you what a website can't

You've researched colleges, scored them through four lenses and started talking with coaches. Now it's time to test your fit in person. A campus visit is where you find out whether a school that looks right on paper feels right: the people, the pace, the place you'd live for four years.

Two kinds of visits

VisitWho paysWhen
UnofficialYour familyAny time. You can tour a campus at any age, though Division I coaches may be limited in what they can discuss before your sport's contact date.
OfficialThe college pays some costsHas a start date by level: most Division I sports August 1 before junior year, Division II June 15 before junior year, Division III January 1 of junior year. The NAIA has no NCAA-style visit calendar.

Most students make several unofficial visits before they're ever offered an official one. Both count. Treat every visit like a chance to learn.

Before you go: prepare your questions

The best visits start at home. Research the program, the roster and your intended major, then write your questions. Plan to talk with four kinds of people:

  • Coaches: how they see you fitting in, what a typical week looks like, how they support academics
  • Admissions and financial aid: what it takes to get in, and what aid is available
  • Professors: what your major is like, and whether athletes can manage its labs and schedule
  • Current students and players: what they love, what's hard, and what they wish they'd known

Email the coach to confirm your visit. Ask whether you can sit in on a class, watch a practice and eat a meal on campus.

On campus: see a normal day

Game days and tour days are polished. You want to see the ordinary days. Attend a class, watch a practice, eat in the dining hall, walk through a dorm. Notice how players talk to each other and to their coaches. Ask yourself the question from Step 6: if I got injured tomorrow and never played here, would I still want to be at this school?

Score it the same day

Every campus starts to blur together after a few visits. Score each one the same day, while it's fresh, using the same six areas every time:

AreaWhat you're judging
AcademicYour major, the classes you saw, academic support for athletes
Personal / SocialCould you picture yourself here, outside your sport?
AthleticThe level, the practice, how players are developed
FinancialWhat you learned about cost and aid
FacilitiesTraining, study spaces, dorms, dining
Coach & programThe coaches, the culture, how the team treats each other

The Campus Visit Scorecard on your dashboard keeps every visit in one place, so when it's time to decide in Step 12, you can compare schools fairly instead of by whichever visit happened last.

After: thank the coach within two days

Send a short thank-you email within two days. Mention something specific you saw or learned, and one reason the visit mattered to you. It's courteous, it keeps the conversation going, and many athletes forget to do it.

Your moves for Step 11

  1. Research the program and write your questions.
  2. Confirm your visit with the coach by email.
  3. Attend a class, a practice and a meal if you can.
  4. Score your visit the same day.
  5. Thank the coach by email within two days.

1My visit questions Prepare · 20 min

Write questions for four kinds of people. Start from the starters, then make them yours.

Coaches

Starters: How do you see me fitting into your program? · What does a typical week look like in season and out? · How do your players balance academics?

Admissions and financial aid

Starters: What does a strong applicant from my school look like? · What merit or need-based aid could I qualify for? · When are the application and aid deadlines?

Professors

Starters: What is my intended major like here? · Can athletes in my sport manage its labs and schedule? · How large are the classes?

Current students and players

Starters: What do you love about this school? · What's hardest about being an athlete here? · What do you wish you'd known before you came?

Preparing your questions ticks milestone S11-M1 on your dashboard's Roadmap. The Campus Visit Edition card deck has a suit of question cards for each group: pick 2–3 per conversation and log the answers.

9Family conversation guide Take-home · 15 min

Dear family, in Step 11 your student-athlete visits campuses: preparing questions, seeing a normal day, scoring each visit the same day and thanking the coach within two days.

The most important idea: visit the campus they'd live on, not just the one on the tour. A class, a practice and a meal tell you more than a brochure.

Do this together

  1. Plan visits around the schools on your student's list, including at least one unofficial visit nearby.
  2. Prepare your own questions for admissions and financial aid.
  3. On the drive home, ask your student to score the visit before you share your opinion.

Your role this step: let your student lead with coaches and current players. Save your questions for admissions and financial aid, where they matter most.

10Coach talking points For head coaches · 5 min read

Say these three things

  1. “Visit the campus you'd live on, not just the tour. Ask to see a class, a practice and a meal.”
  2. “Score every visit the same day, the same way, so you can compare fairly.”
  3. “Thank the coach within two days. It keeps the conversation going.”

Questions you'll get

QuestionAnswer
“Should I visit if they haven't contacted me?”Yes. Unofficial visits are open to anyone, and showing up shows interest.
“What should I ask the coach?”How they see you fitting in, what a typical week looks like, and how players handle academics.
“Can you call ahead for me?”Offer to let the college coach know they're visiting and to share your read on the player.
