Be Recruitable: Through a College Coach's Eyes

By Coach Stack · College Recruiting Standard

Draft for Coach Stack's review. New content written for Lesson 7, built on the framework's Step 7 actions, the four areas college coaches evaluate, and the Sport Wheel in O.W.N. ID.

See yourself the way a college coach does

Welcome to Level IV, Identity & Presence. Up to now, you've been learning about the landscape, yourself and the colleges that might fit you. Now the view flips. How does a college coach see you?

College coaches don't just watch highlights. They're trying to answer one question: will this person make my team better for four years, on the field and off it? To answer it, they look at four areas.

The four areas college coaches evaluate

AreaWhat a coach looks at
AcademicsYour grades, your core courses and whether you'll be eligible and admitted (everything from Step 5)
AthleticsYour technical skills and your tactical understanding: how well you play, and how well you read the game
Physical & AthleticismSpeed, strength, size for your position, endurance and how your body is developing
FitCharacter, attitude and coachability, and how you'd fit their team, their style and their culture

Notice that only one of the four is about skill alone. Coaches recruit people, not just players. A slightly less talented player with great grades and a great attitude often gets the spot.

Rate yourself honestly

Start with your own ratings. In O.W.N. ID's Sport Wheel, you rate yourself on eight things college coaches look at in your sport: six sport-specific skills plus mental toughness and attitude and coachability, which matter in every sport. Rate yourself for the level you're playing at right now, from Developing to Solid, Strong or Standout.

Be honest. A wheel full of “Standout” doesn't help you. A wheel that shows where you really are gives you somewhere to go.

Then ask your coach to rate you the same way

Here's where it gets useful. Ask your high school or club coach to rate you on the same wheel. Then put the two side by side.

Where your ratings match, you see yourself clearly. Where they're different is the conversation you need to have. Maybe you rated your first touch as Strong and your coach said Solid. Ask why. Ask what Strong looks like. That one conversation can teach you more than a season of guessing.

Talk about the biggest gap

Pick the biggest difference between your rating and your coach's. Ask three questions:

  1. What do you see that I'm not seeing?
  2. What would it look like if I were one level higher?
  3. What's one thing I could work on this month?

Listen. Don't argue. Thank your coach. Then write it down.

Set one development goal in each area

Now set one goal in each of the four areas: one academic, one athletic, one physical and one about fit, such as being the most coachable player at practice. Make them small, specific and scheduled, just like the habits you built in Step 4. You have time: most recruiting conversations really get going in junior year, and the work you do now is what coaches will see then.

Register, if you need to

If you're considering NCAA Division I or II, you'll need an account with the NCAA Eligibility Center. If you made a free Profile Page in Step 2, ask your counselor when to upgrade it to a Certification Account, which has a fee (waivers are available for eligible students). If you're considering the NAIA, it has its own eligibility center, called PlayNAIA, and you register there separately. Division III and junior college students don't need either.

Your moves for Step 7

  1. Rate yourself in all four areas.
  2. Ask your coach to rate you the same way.
  3. Discuss the biggest gap with your coach.
  4. Register with the NCAA or NAIA Eligibility Center, if needed.
  5. Set one development goal per area in your Tracker.

9Family conversation guide Take-home · 15 min

Dear family, your student-athlete has started Level IV, Identity & Presence. In Step 7 they learn to see themselves the way college coaches do, in four areas: academics, athletics, physical and athleticism, and fit. They'll rate themselves, ask their coach to rate them the same way, and set one goal in each area.

The most important idea: the gap between how they see themselves and how their coach sees them is where growth happens.

Questions to ask your student

  1. Which of the four areas is your strongest? Which needs the most work?
  2. What did your coach's rating show that surprised you?
  3. What's your goal in each area?
  4. Do you need to register with the NCAA or NAIA? When?

Your role this step: let your student hear their coach's honest view, even if it's hard. Avoid stepping in to argue a rating. Help with the practical parts: training time, rides, and the eligibility registration fee if it applies.

Fit is family business too: college coaches notice how parents behave at games. Supporting the coach in public helps your student.

10Coach talking points For head coaches · 5 min read

Say these three things

  1. “College coaches look at four areas: academics, athletics, physical and fit. Only one is about skill alone.”
  2. “Rate yourself honestly. Then I'll rate you the same way, and we'll talk about the biggest gap.”
  3. “Set one goal in every area. You have until junior year to show growth.”
