ARTICLE · ABOUT 7 MINUTES · DRAFT

Commit to the Process: Habits That Hold Up

By Coach Stack · College Recruiting Standard

Draft for Coach Stack's review. New content written for Lesson 4, built on the framework's Step 4 actions, the Wheel of Recruiting Success and the Goal Stack Planner.

Motivation gets you started. Habits keep you going.

In Step 3 you found your why, built your goal pyramid and wrote your identity statement. That's the top of the pyramid. Step 4 is about the base: the small things you do every week that make the big goal real.

Here's the honest truth about motivation: it comes and goes. You'll feel fired up after a great game or a good talk with a coach. You'll feel flat after a loss, a bad test or a long week. If your progress depends on how you feel, it will stall. Habits don't care how you feel. That's why they work.

Start with an honest check: retake the Wheel

Back in Step 1 you took the Wheel of Recruiting Success as your Baseline. It rated eight areas of your life as a student-athlete, from athletic development and academics to time management, your support system and mental toughness.

Now retake it and put the two side by side. Where did you grow? Where did you slip? Which area is lowest right now? Your lowest area is usually where a new habit will help the most.

One habit for each commitment

Your goal pyramid covers four commitments. In Step 4, you choose one habit for each:

CommitmentWhat it coversA habit might look like
Your sportSkills, strength, film, recovery20 minutes of position work on my own, three days a week
Your academicsGrades, study habits, staying eligibleCheck my grades in the portal every Sunday night
Your recruitingResearch, organization, later outreachResearch one college program in depth each week
YourselfSleep, balance, the people who matterEight hours of sleep on school nights

One habit per commitment. Not five. You can add more later, once these four are automatic.

Make it small, specific and scheduled

“Work harder in school” isn't a habit. It's a wish. A real habit answers three questions: what exactly, when, and for how long?

  • Too vague: “Get better at soccer.”
  • Ready to go: “Twenty minutes of first-touch work in the backyard, Monday, Wednesday and Friday after dinner.”

Then block the time in your weekly schedule, the same way practice is on the calendar. If it doesn't have a time, it doesn't happen.

Track it for four weeks

Check off your habits every day for four weeks in the Goal Stack Planner. Four weeks is long enough to hit a busy stretch, a tough game week or a big test, and that's the point. Anyone can keep a habit when life is easy.

You will miss a day. Everyone does. The rule is simple: miss once, get back on track the next day. One missed day is a slip. Two in a row starts a new pattern.

Plan for the busy weeks

Your season, your schoolwork and later your recruiting will all get busy at the same time. Decide now what you'll do when that happens. An “if, then” plan helps: “If I have a game on a school night, then I'll do ten minutes of review on the bus instead of thirty at home.” A smaller version of the habit still counts.

Commitment is a mindset

Grit isn't about never struggling. It's about coming back after you struggle. When a habit slips, don't decide you're “bad at this.” Look at what got in the way, adjust the plan, and start again tomorrow. That's what committing to the process means.

These routines also start the Three Habits you'll use at every level of this journey: Be Informed & Organized, Be Realistic & Resilient and Prioritize Health & Wellness.

Your moves for Step 4

  1. Retake the Wheel of Recruiting Success and compare it with your Baseline.
  2. Choose one habit for each commitment.
  3. Block time for each habit in your weekly schedule.
  4. Check off your habits every day for four weeks.
  5. Review your progress with a coach or counselor.

3Too vague or ready to go? Sort · 5 min

Is each habit Ready to go (what, when and how long are clear) or Too vague?

1. “Study more.”

No what, no when, no how long.

2. “Review my notes for 20 minutes before bed on Monday, Tuesday and Thursday.”

Specific, scheduled and small enough to do.

3. “Get stronger.”

Which exercises, which days, how long?

4. “Three 30-minute lifts a week: Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday mornings.”

It says exactly what and when.

5. “Be better about recruiting.”

What would you actually do this week?

6. “Research one college program in depth every Sunday afternoon.”

One clear action, one set time.

7. “Sleep more.”

How much, and what has to change to get it?

8. “Phone on the charger in the kitchen by 10:15 on school nights.”

A specific action that makes eight hours possible.

8Family conversation guide Take-home · 15 min

Dear family, this week your student-athlete is turning the goals they set in Step 3 into weekly habits: one each for their sport, their academics, their recruiting and themselves. They'll track those habits for four weeks.

The most important idea: motivation comes and goes, habits keep you going. Small, specific, scheduled habits hold up during busy seasons.

Questions to ask your student

  1. What are your four habits? When in the week will you do each one?
  2. Which Wheel area was your lowest? What habit will help it?
  3. What's the busiest night of your week, and what's your plan for it?
  4. How can we help? (A quiet study spot, a ride, a phone-free hour?)

Your role this step: support the schedule, not the scoreboard. Ask about the habit, not the result. If a week goes badly, help your student figure out what got in the way rather than adding pressure.

Protect sleep: it's one of the habits that helps everything else.

9Coach talking points For head coaches · 5 min read

Say these three things

  1. “Motivation gets you started. Habits keep you going. Pick one habit for your sport, your schoolwork, your recruiting and yourself.”
  2. “Make it small, specific and scheduled. If it doesn't have a time, it doesn't happen.”
  3. “You'll miss a day. Miss once, then get back on track.”
