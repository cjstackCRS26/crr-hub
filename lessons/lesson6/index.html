ARTICLE · ABOUT 7 MINUTES · DRAFT

Find Your Best Fit: Four Lenses, One Decision

By Coach Stack · College Recruiting Standard

Draft for Coach Stack's review. New content written for Lesson 6, built on the framework's Step 6 actions and the College Fit Tool's four lenses.

The biggest name isn't the best fit

Ask a room of freshmen where they want to play and you'll hear the same dozen schools, the ones on TV. But the right college for you might be one you've never heard of. Fit matters more than the name on the jersey.

In Step 2 you learned how big the landscape is. In Step 5 you learned what each level asks of you academically. Step 6 brings it together: you start judging real colleges, one at a time, through four lenses.

Four lenses, in this order

LensThe questionWhat you look at
1. Academic fitCan I get in, and can I study what I want here?Admitted students' grades, your major, whether athletes in your sport can take it, academic support
2. Athletic fitIs this my level, and do they need someone like me?The roster, their needs in your class, their style of play, whether a coach has shown interest
3. Financial fitWhat does this actually cost my family?The net price after aid, how athletic aid works in your sport, what your family can afford
4. Personal fitWould I be happy here as a person?Size, setting, distance from home, social life outside your sport

Why academics first? Because your degree lasts a lifetime and your playing career doesn't. And here's the question that cuts through everything: if you got injured tomorrow and never played a game there, would you still want to be at that school?

Score real colleges

Pick three colleges and score each one through all four lenses in the College Fit Tool. Choose three different kinds of school, not three versions of your dream school. The tool shows you where each one is strong and where it's weak, so you can compare them side by side.

Get an honest read on your level

Athletic fit is the hardest lens to judge yourself. You'll need someone who has watched you play and will tell you the truth. Ask your high school or club coach: “Based on what you've seen, what level do you think I could play at right now, and what would move me up?”

The answer might not be what you hoped. That's useful. An honest read now saves you from spending two years chasing schools that were never realistic, and from overlooking ones that are.

Find the real price

The sticker price on a college website is not what most families pay. Every college that offers federal financial aid must post a net price calculator on its website. Sit down with your family and run it for each school you score. It takes about 15 minutes and gives you an estimate of what you'd actually pay after grants and scholarships.

Two things to remember: most athletic aid is partial, and Division III offers none, though academic and need-based aid can be generous. Ask whether your family could afford a school with no athletic money at all.

See a campus

Tour at least one campus, in person or online. Walk around. Picture a normal Tuesday there, not a game day. Does it feel like a place you could live for four years?

Build your first target list

Now put together a first list of five or more colleges across at least two levels: Division II and Division III, for example, or Division I and the NAIA. A list that spans levels keeps doors open while you're still growing. It will change many times before you choose, and that's the point.

When you save a school in the College Fit Tool, it goes straight to My College List on your dashboard.

Your moves for Step 6

  1. Score three colleges in the College Fit Tool.
  2. Ask your coach for an honest evaluation of your level.
  3. Run the net price calculator on each college's website.
  4. Tour a campus, in person or online.
  5. Add your initial target list to your Tracker.

9Family conversation guide Take-home · 20 min

Dear family, in Step 6 your student-athlete starts judging real colleges through four lenses: academic, athletic, financial and personal. They'll score three colleges, ask their coach for an honest read of their level, and build a first list of five or more schools across at least two levels.

The most important idea: fit matters more than the name. A school that fits in all four ways is one your student is more likely to finish and enjoy.

Do this together

  1. Run the net price calculator on one or two of your student's colleges. It needs family income information, so your student will need your help.
  2. Ask: could we afford this school if there were no athletic money at all?
  3. Talk about distance: how far is too far for you as a family?

Questions to ask your student

  1. Which lens matters most to you, and why?
  2. What did your coach say about your level? How do you feel about it?
  3. Would you still want to go to your top school if you never played a game there?

Your role this step: share real numbers and real limits early. It's kinder to name a budget now than after your student falls in love with a school.

10Coach talking points For head coaches · 5 min read

Say these three things

  1. “Look at every college through four lenses: academic, athletic, financial and personal. Academics first.”
  2. “Ask me for an honest read of your level. I'll tell you the truth, and what would move you up.”
  3. “Build a list across at least two levels. It keeps doors open while you're still growing.”

When a player asks for your honest read

DoAvoid
Name a range of levels, with one or two reasonsA single label, like “you're a D1 player”
Say what would move them up, specificallyVague praise or vague criticism
Suggest a few programs at those levels to researchPromising anything about a college's interest
Offer to revisit it in six monthsTreating it as final
