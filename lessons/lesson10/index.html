ARTICLE · ABOUT 7 MINUTES · DRAFT

Be Seen: From Identified to Recruited

By Coach Stack · College Recruiting Standard

Draft for Coach Stack's review. New content written for Lesson 10, built on the framework's Step 10 actions and the ID Event Tracker. The five funnel stages are a draft wording of the identified-versus-recruited idea.

Coaches can only recruit students they know about

You can have great grades, a strong brand and well-written emails, but at some point a college coach has to see you play. Step 10 is about making that happen on purpose: choosing the right events, telling coaches you'll be there, playing your best when it counts and following up afterward.

Identified is not the same as recruited

Many athletes are identified but never recruited. A coach knowing your name is a start, not a finish. Think of recruiting as a funnel. Lots of athletes enter at the top, and fewer move down each stage:

StageWhat it looks like
1. IdentifiedThe coach knows your name: from a questionnaire, an email or a list
2. EvaluatedThe coach watches you play, in person or on film
3. RecruitedThe coach pursues you: calls, personal messages, an invitation to visit
4. OfferedThe coach offers you a roster spot, and maybe aid
5. CommittedYou choose, and they choose you

Every school on your list sits somewhere in your funnel. Your job in Level V is to move the right schools further down. Getting seen is how you move from identified to evaluated, and what you do once you're seen decides whether you go further.

Three elements of exposure

Bring energy. Coaches notice effort and attitude in the first few minutes, often before they notice skill. Warm up like it matters, communicate with teammates, and compete on every play, including the ones that don't go your way.

Get on the radar. Coaches go where they expect to see players they're already interested in. Pick events where the schools on your list will actually be, and tell those coaches you're coming before the event.

Shine when it counts. Prepare like it's a big game: rest, eat well, know your schedule, bring what you need. Then play your game. Don't try to do something you never do in practice just because a coach is watching.

Choose events on purpose

Camps, showcases, tournaments, combines and clinics can be expensive, and not every event is worth it. Before you sign up, ask:

  • Will coaches from schools on my list actually be there?
  • Have I contacted those coaches yet?
  • What does it cost, for each school that will see me?
  • How is it run? Will I get real playing time in front of coaches?
  • Am I ready: registered, film sent, schools researched, goals set?

The ID Event Tracker turns those questions into an Event Worth score out of 100, and tells you what would raise it. A camp at a single college is often the best way to be evaluated by that college's staff.

Email coaches before each event

A coach at a showcase may be watching dozens of fields. An email the week before, with the event, your field or court, your team, your number and your schedule, tells them exactly where to look. The Communication Lab has an event schedule email for this.

Ask your coach to help

Your high school or club coach can contact college programs for you, and college coaches often trust a coach's word. Ask yours to reach out to two or three schools on your list, and give them your profile and schedule to share.

Follow up after every event

After the event, email every coach you contacted. Thank them, mention something specific from the event, and ask a question about next steps. This is where many athletes drop off, and where you can stand out. The ID Event Tracker reminds you which follow-ups are still due.

Your moves for Step 10

  1. Choose events where your target schools recruit.
  2. Email those coaches your schedule before each event.
  3. Ask your coach to contact programs for you.
  4. Follow up with every coach after an event.
  5. Track where each school is in your funnel.
“Getting noticed gets you in the funnel. How you compete, and how you follow up, moves you through it.”Coach Stack

10Family conversation guide Take-home · 15 min

Dear family, in Step 10 your student-athlete works on being seen: choosing events where the schools on their list will be, emailing coaches before, competing well and following up after.

The most important idea: being identified isn't the same as being recruited. Camp emails and questionnaires are a start. Moving a school further down the funnel takes the right events and follow-through.

Do this together

  1. Look at the event calendar and the cost together. Use the ID Event Tracker's score before paying for an event.
  2. Ask: which schools on your list will actually be there?
  3. Help with the logistics: registration, travel, rest the night before.

Your role this step: at events, be calm and supportive from the sideline. Coaches notice parents too. Let your student talk to coaches themselves.

Budget note: events add up quickly. One well-chosen camp at a college on the list can be worth more than several big showcases.

11Coach talking points For head coaches · 5 min read

Say these three things

  1. “Getting identified isn't the same as getting recruited. Pick events where your list schools will be.”
  2. “Tell coaches you're coming before every event, and follow up after.”
  3. “Bring energy from warm-ups on. Coaches watch everything.”
