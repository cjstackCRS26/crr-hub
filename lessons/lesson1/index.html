ARTICLE · ABOUT 8 MINUTES

Own the Process: Why Your Recruiting Journey Starts With You

By Coach Stack · College Recruiting Standard

Nobody is going to do this for you

Here is the first thing to know about college recruiting, and the thing most families learn too late: you run your recruiting journey. Adults support you, but no one can do it for you.

That can sound like pressure. It isn't meant to. It's meant to be freeing. It means the process isn't something that happens to you while you wait to be discovered. It's something you do, one email, one question and one deadline at a time.

The recruiting journey can feel overwhelming, confusing and even frustrating at times. It doesn't have to be. Recruiting is an active, ongoing process, and the more time and effort you put into it, the better your results will be. Every later step builds on this one, and the habits you build now will last all four years of high school.

No one owes you a college offer. But you owe it to yourself to show what you're capable of, on and off the field.

Coaches recruit students, not parents

College coaches notice who sends the email.

When you write to a coach, ask the question and track the deadline, the coach sees initiative, and initiative is part of what they evaluate. A message written by a parent tells a coach something very different from one written by the athlete it's about. Coaches are picturing who you'll be on their campus, managing your own schedule, your own classes and your own season. The way you run your recruiting is an early look at that.

This doesn't mean your family steps back. It means everyone takes the right seat.

Three types of recruits: which one are you?

Not every student-athlete approaches recruiting the same way. Some take control. Others coast. And some wait around, hoping it all just happens. Where you stand in the process will directly affect where you land.

The Intentional RecruitProactive · Educated · Organized · Driven

Owns the process. They don't wait for things to happen; they make them happen. They understand the landscape, stay organized with trackers and college lists, reach out to coaches with purpose, build their brand, ask questions and stay consistent. These are the recruits coaches want to hear from, and they stand out not just because of talent but because of effort, awareness and maturity.

The Casual RecruitInconsistent · Reactive · Underdirected

Wants to be recruited but doesn't put in the full work. They might email a few coaches but not follow up. They go to showcases but don't prepare. They're in the game, but not fully invested, hoping things fall into place.

The Passive RecruitUnprepared · Unmotivated · Misguided

Believes talent alone will get them recruited: "If I'm good enough, coaches will find me." They rarely take initiative, expect others to do the work and miss key opportunities. Even talented athletes get overlooked this way, because they never truly show up in the process.

Here's the good news: you can level up at any time. If you're not where you want to be, you can change. It takes commitment, effort and a clear plan. A Passive Recruit in September can be an Intentional Recruit by spring.

The map: Seven Levels, Thirteen Steps, Three Habits

Owning the process starts with seeing all of it. The College Recruiting Readiness journey runs through Seven Levels and Thirteen Steps, from your first fall of high school to your first day of college:

  1. Launch (Steps 1–2): own the process and learn the landscape of college athletics.
  2. Discover (Steps 3–4): find your purpose and commit to the process.
  3. Pathways (Steps 5–6): understand the power of academics and find your best fit.
  4. Identity & Presence (Steps 7–8): become recruitable and build your brand.
  5. Exposure & Engagement (Steps 9–11): share your story, be seen and visit campus.
  6. Align & Commit (Step 12): weigh your options and select your best fit.
  7. Making the Transition (Step 13): prepare for college life.

There are two checkpoints along the way. Recruit Ready comes after Level IV, by the start of Grade 11, so you're prepared before junior year, when college coaches become most active. College Bound Ready comes at graduation, and it counts whether you choose a college roster or another path. The goal is a well-prepared, well-made decision, not one particular outcome.

Running underneath all seven Levels are the Three Habits: be informed and organized, be realistic and resilient, and prioritize health and wellness. The Levels are the road. The Habits are how you travel it.

Along the way you're becoming a SMART Recruit: Strategic, Motivated, Authentic, Resilient and Targeted. Step 1's focus is Motivated, because ownership starts with the decision to act. A SMART Recruit doesn't wait around to be discovered. They build a plan and take intentional action.

Know where you stand: the Baseline

You can't plan a route without knowing your starting point. That's what the Baseline is for.

The Baseline is the Wheel of Recruiting Success: eight areas, four questions each, and every one of them is something you control. It asks what you're doing (training beyond practice, asking for feedback, planning your week, knowing your GPA) and never how good an athlete you are. Your coaches judge talent. The Wheel measures the part that's yours.

There's no total score and no verdict. The shape shows you where to work next, because a wheel only rolls as well as its flattest side. And if you're in ninth grade and your numbers are low, that's the correct result, not a bad one. Take it now, take it again at the end of every Level, and watch the shape change.

Your five moves this week

Ownership isn't a feeling. It's a set of actions. Here are Step 1's:

  1. Complete your orientation and find Level I.
  2. Submit your Intent to Play form to your head coach.
  3. Take the Baseline.
  4. Record your results in your Tracker.
  5. Tell your family how they can support you.

Step 1 is done when you've submitted the Intent to Play form, taken the Baseline and opened your Tracker. Check each one off on your Level I tab.

Then sit with one question before you move on: what will you own, starting this week? If you want to go further, ask yourself what excites you most about the recruiting process and what worries you, what three things you can start doing this week, and who you can go to for guidance.
